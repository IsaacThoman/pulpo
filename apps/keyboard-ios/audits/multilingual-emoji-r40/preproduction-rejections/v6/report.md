## Definitive posting-prefix lemma finding

**The complete-query posting branch is valid**, independently of the failed exact branch. Let Q be nonempty actual `normalized(q)` and K an actual final retained indexed key with `K.hasPrefix(Q)`.

Swift's published concrete implementation compares NFC UTF8 prefixes **and checks the ending grapheme boundary**, or falls back to `starts(with:)` on Characters. Its documented comparison is on Unicode extended grapheme clusters. The pinned primary source is retained in `challenge/StringLegacy-pinned.swift`, with URL, commit and hash in `challenge/prefix-source.json`: [Swift StringLegacy.swift, commit 453277e](https://github.com/swiftlang/swift/blob/453277eb74bf700b9558dabd8261a264ee7e4f2e/stdlib/public/core/StringLegacy.swift). This upstream source is not claimed to be the installed Apple binary's exact build source. Actual host probes confirm the relevant concrete boundary behavior, including `("a"+U+0903).hasPrefix("a")==false` and `(U+0D4E+SPACE).hasPrefix(U+0D4E)==false`. Finite probes are corroboration, not the universal proof.

A true Character-prefix relation preserves every delimiter position when both strings are split on Character exactly equal to ASCII SPACE, as baseline does. Every complete Q word equals the corresponding K word; Q's last word is a Character prefix of its corresponding K word. Copying these words to Strings does not change the partition: standalone SPACE boundaries reset grapheme context. A prepend+space or space+mark cluster that is not exactly the delimiter Character was never split and remains inside a word. Canonical-equivalent Character equality preserves this delimiter predicate. Therefore every word in W, and hence every g in G(W), prefixes a spaceword of K, which is explicitly included in V for an entry retained at K.

Normalization's final whitespace split/join ensures nonempty Q starts with no standalone SPACE Character. Consequently its first **positional** word starts at K's beginning and prefixes K. That word belongs to W, proving the existential posting anchor. This is not necessarily the first member of sorted W. The leading-delimiter premise matters: the unnormalized raw-string statement would fail for K=Q=`" a"`; that input cannot be the nonempty final normalized output.

The fallback posting branch is also directly source-valid: a returned fallback entry originates in an actual per-nonignored-word `collect` result, giving its retained-key anchor; source then explicitly checks every nonignored query word against `searchWords`, a subset of V. The 800-candidate fallback bound, rank thresholds, whole-query early occupancy, caps, and preference leads only remove/reorder potential results and do not invalidate these membership implications. Final posting truncation is represented by reflected actual retained keys, not full vocabulary. No transfer through Spanish normalization is used in either posting proof.

These findings establish source branches (2) and (3), not overall completeness. Branch (1) fails because raw whitespace squeezing and scalar apostrophe deletion do not commute across grapheme boundaries. No claim about Foundation projection transport, every Unicode scalar, or previous rejected same-T identities repairs it.
