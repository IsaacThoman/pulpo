# R38 independent native postreveal claims review

**Disposition: meaningful native privacy gains supported; no unaddressed production mapping defect found.** Native secure-transition text/selection preservation remains failed and unaccepted. The evidence supports a native-host/oracle limitation for this change, not a mapping regression or an all-green production claim.

Reviewed the frozen contract/candidate, source/domain reviews, seal/reveal records, original rejected blind artifacts, six objections and author responses, validated v2 review, calibration, runner/adapter/controller source, focused native results, and paired heldout archives/comparison. This review performed only local source/artifact inspection and report writing: no project execution, tests, builds, devices, network, candidate/oracle changes, or corpus exclusions. Historical blindness and run provenance remain recorded claims, not independently witnessed history; parent publishing validation is still required.

## Native secure-selection finding

In `heldout-native-{baseline,candidate}.json.gz`, `r38-secure-trait-transitions` begins correctly selected. At zero-based step 1, the normal-to-secure `field` operation retains the text but expands the selection to the entire document in **both** versions: en_US `[5,4] → [0,15]`, es `[4,4] → [0,12]`, en_es `[5,4] → [0,13]`. Subsequent typing therefore replaces the whole selected document: en_US becomes `meadow`, rather than `left|meadow|right`. The later text/caret differences cascade from this first mismatch. Each locale retains 15 failures: one initial selection failure, then seven text/selection pairs, totaling **45 native-only assertions**.

The real observed failure must not be dismissed because the intended source behavior preserves text. `R38NativeDocument.apply` assigns `UITextView.isSecureTextEntry`, other traits and content type, then reconciles the actual `PreviewHost`/`TextViewDocument`; it does not restore selection. Initialization instead applies traits before installing text/selection. The transition observation is consistent with a UIKit secure-setter side effect. Snapshots bracket the complete field/reconcile operation, however, so they do not experimentally isolate that setter or establish a universal UIKit rule.

Both versions have identical secure-case text, selections, policy and failures; secure override already existed, and this candidate only adds exact content-type strings. Production code does not introduce this setter. Thus there is no evidence of a newly introduced mapping/editing defect. There is concrete failure of the frozen native selection-preservation expectation in this fixture, including actual prefix/suffix loss on subsequent typing. Keep it failed; do not claim secure-transition preservation, silently restore selection, retune the oracle, or generalize this unattached UITextView exercise to all production hosts. The core secure cases pass, which cannot erase the native observation.

## Valid claims and boundaries

- **Exact mapping:** calibration uses 45 named UIKit constants through UITextView → PreviewHost → actual `InputTraits(UITextInputTraits)`, with raw/adapter equality. Baseline recognizes 2/24 private categories; candidate recognizes 24/24 and leaves 21 named normal controls nonprivate. The set adds exactly the 22 missing measured values, including `creditCardMiddleName → cc-additional-name`, retaining all 24 aliases. Exact membership, opaque custom strings, secure override and existing glide logic remain intact. Nil and all normal exceptions are exercised separately in heldout evidence. The production patch is confined to `InputTraits.swift`; no Engine/AppModel/store/Emoji/resource change is needed.
- **Native heldout result:** 18 → **48/54** case-language passes, 30 gains, 18 common passes and 6 common failures across en_US/es/en_es; 1,272 transitions remain recorded. Core is 21 → 51/54. Native candidate retains **57 assertions**: the 45 above plus 12 touch-positive failures shared with core. No previously passing whole case becomes failing. This is not assertion/transition-level nonregression: the normal-learning case has three baseline-pass/candidate-fail final transitions, and newly failing touch-positive assertions after the private commit. Baseline touch positivity there comes from unauthorized private-word learning; it cannot certify the earlier normal touch control.
- **Behavioral gains survive those failures:** native private groups pass complete UTF16 text/caret/selection checks on standard and numeric profiles. For example, private email input after space has baseline totalCount 1/touchWeight 6 versus candidate 0/0 with matching literal text. Normal `garden`/`escuela`/`hello` readbacks reach count/total 1; the distinct private word stays zero in the candidate. Normal touch positivity remains unaccepted, and empty pair readbacks do not establish a positive pair-learning control. Separate focused native normal touch controls passed; they do not make the blind control pass.
- **Direct guards:** all 39 native challenges fire; 18 candidate confirmations are explicit available refusals and **21 automatic challenges are unavailable**, because TextViewDocument has no stable ID. Those 21 earn no safety credit. The late-private confirmation actually inserts on baseline and refuses on candidate in all three languages, independently of missing ID. Standard/numeric destination checks prevent keyboard classification from masquerading as privacy. Guard challenges are not controller or audio acceptance.
- **Actual controller:** final focused evidence is baseline 3 failed, candidate 4 passed including calibration. Forced hidden microphone callbacks use Full Access, synthetic signed-in/dictation-enabled account and a temporary bridge, reaching the privacy check; baseline actually creates requests/start commands, while candidate reports the specific privacy refusal with neither. Private recovery offers no callback, so it is unavailable rather than a fired refusal. The previously captured normal recovery callback is actually fired after entering email privacy and preserves text/caret/delivery state; restored-normal recovery actually inserts and marks delivered. This supports the tested controller routes, not recording or remote service success.

The retained pre-reveal repairs resolve unsupported normal commit/count assumptions, stale-choice dispatch, private-origin permanence, transcript spacing and punctuation-through-type. They do not retrospectively validate those removed claims: v2 has six successful actual literal captures but no stale `choose`, and no allowed nonempty transcript insertion. Separate capability smoke records 105 transitions/zero failures on each platform and 18 deliberate errors retained on each; capability admission is not heldout success.
