## What can be retained

A complete envelope replaced by one successful rename, with payload and reset epochs in that same envelope, can linearize the durable two-store clear. A stable, never-unlinked sidecar lock can serialize cooperating transactions across processes. `LOCK_NB`, no time-based lock stealing, fresh reconciliation under lock, independent store epochs, and preserving contacts/replacements are appropriate foundations. The explicit exclusion of power-loss directory persistence is honest. Background saving must remain enabled. Unrelated current-epoch late learning must merge rather than be discarded wholesale.

These conclusions assume every writer participates in the protocol, all aliases choose the same authority/lock, and no protected state is modified before a failed transaction is abandoned. They do not approve the current complete contract.

## Required corrections

### R1 — Reject the unbounded historical forget map; select an exact bounded alternative

Lines 7, 15, and 19 do not establish a metadata bound. Renaming a growing historical map “user choices” does not bound it. A local 256-record cap does not bound distinct keys forgotten over the lifetime of the authority, particularly after unblocking and pruning.

Counterexample: writer A holds a pending learn of X at revision r. Other writers forget X, relearn X, and later prune it. Deleting X's watermark then admits A's stale increment. Keeping every watermark while A can be suspended indefinitely permits arbitrarily many distinct forgotten/relearned keys. A floor based only on the oldest writer revision retains an unbounded number of intervening tombstones. TTLs, revision-age limits, LRU, a fixed tombstone cache, or epoch rotation that drops all pending writers fail the required exactness or preservation requirement. Merely choosing SQLite does not prove bounded history or safe compaction.

**Recommended replacement for the next preflight: a fixed-capacity durable outstanding-operation registrar, described below.** This moves negative-history retention to the finite set of operations that actually can arrive late. It is a change to the admission contract, including real filesystem work per accepted kept word; it needs explicit acceptance and performance evidence in the next review. It is not permission to implement from this review.

A workable exact design is:

1. Fix a finite writer-slot count P and at most 256 outstanding dictionary operations plus 256 outstanding touch operations per slot. For example P=8 gives at most 4096 operation records across the authority, not 256 times an unbounded number of objects/processes. Choose and freeze actual limits, not the example. Multiple in-process instances share the same bounded authority coordinator rather than creating unlimited queues/registrars.
2. Give each writer slot a stable, never-replaced ownership sidecar held by an exclusive OS lock for the writer lifetime. Use a new slot-incarnation identifier and monotonic operation sequence. A contender may reclaim a slot only after nonblocking acquisition of its lifetime lock, proving the former owner no longer holds it. Never reclaim a suspended owner's slot by time, PID reuse, revision age, or reset. Verify actual same-process and separate-process lock semantics; a process-local coordinator must prevent self-reclamation and shared-descriptor reentrancy.
3. Before reporting a new persisted-domain learn/pin accepted, under the global authority lock observe the current epoch and durably register an operation token, normalized target key, epoch, and bounded status. Keep its actual touch samples only in the local bounded queue. A successful registration is admission, not a durable count increment. A crash can lose locally pending best-effort learning, as in the existing app; do not promise crash-recoverable uncommitted samples. Registration failure teaches nothing for that new operation and returns an explicit failure. Busy observation cannot create an unregistered deferred operation whose key the registrar cannot protect. In particular, blanket “teach in last observed epoch” on busy must be removed for new unregistered keys.
4. Serialize ordinary forget as a durable transaction before returning accepted: remove forms and target pairs, set the block, and mark exactly all already-registered pending learn/pin tokens for that normalized key revoked in the same replacement. Pending operations for every other key remain valid. This avoids a permanent historical watermark entirely. A subsequent learn/pin registers after that forget and may unblock on application. If a future contract instead queues forget, it must specify equivalent token invalidation and local dependency ordering, not revive the revision-only rule.
5. At save execution, validate each token/incarnation/epoch/status against the latest registrar. Apply a pending token once and atomically change its status to committed with the payload. Replays of committed or revoked tokens are no-ops. Keep bounded terminal statuses until acknowledgement, or supply an equally exact bounded acknowledgement/high-water protocol; never free/reuse an identity in a way that can admit an old queued save. An explicit key forget may revoke its matching token; a store reset may revoke that store's tokens. Neither action revokes unrelated current-epoch tokens.
6. A dead writer's slot can be cleared after acquiring its ownership lock because no cooperating live process can later replay that incarnation. Reuse changes incarnation. A suspended writer retains its slot and outstanding tokens indefinitely. When all slots/records are occupied, reject admission explicitly with no new local teaching; do not evict unrelated valid operations. This is bounded safety and backpressure, not unconditional learning availability. Background saves of existing valid tokens still work and preserve their data. A reset need not wait for ownership locks: it can revoke relevant tokens under the global transaction lock.
7. Acquire lifetime-slot locks nonblockingly before transaction ownership; never wait for a slot while holding the global authority lock. Reclamation, admission, status changes, and payload changes must be globally serialized. Bound scans by P and the fixed record limits. Touch tokens need no word/key or persisted sample sequence, only opaque identity/epoch/status.

Why this is exact: any accepted learn that might arrive late has a registered identity. A forget sees and revokes all earlier registered operations for its target in its own serialized transaction. An operation registered after that forget is causally later and survives. No unrelated token is revoked, even after arbitrary forgets of other keys. Once no matching pending token exists, there is no stale operation requiring historical tombstones. No wall-clock lease proof is needed. Serial application plus token status prevents double counts on retries. There is no unbounded mutation history.

Bounds must cover bytes as well as records. “40 characters” is not a UTF-8 byte bound: a grapheme can contain arbitrarily many combining marks. Freeze byte limits for stored spellings, normalized registrar keys, and pair history, or provide another exact size bound. Over-limit admission returns false without truncation. A finite hash alone is not exact normalized-key equality unless collisions are explicitly resolved. This is a compatibility/admission decision that must be stated, not hidden in implementation.

Existing `blocked` is separate product state and is currently unbounded. Explicitly say whether retained explicit blocks are permitted user data rather than newly bounded metadata. If the requirement includes total authority size, an additional block-capacity/admission policy is necessary; exact permanent blocking of arbitrarily many distinct keys cannot fit in fixed bytes. Do not silently evict blocks or count them as a solved metadata bound.

### R2 — Define observation, local causality, and read guarantees

Lines 11 and 15 mix observation linearization, deferred application, and local optimistic state. Revision-only eligibility fails even without another process: A observes revision r, queues `forget(X)`, then queues `learn(X)` while durable revision is still r. Replaying the forget at r+1 makes the later learn's observed revision r fail the stated eligibility test. The new contract must either commit forget synchronously as above or encode and honor local predecessor dependencies. Cover pin-after-local-forget, multiple forget/relearn cycles in one batch, and case/accent variants sharing a normalized key.

Define separately: admitted, locally visible, durable, revoked, busy, capacity, and I/O-invalid outcomes. A learning Bool by itself cannot distinguish successful observation from “accepted using stale epoch”; callers may ignore a return value, but the protocol and public test surface cannot conflate those states. State precisely which operations are speculative and where each operation takes effect. Do not claim strict serializability of every successful public learning call if its durable effects are deferred and can later be revoked by an observed reset/forget.

On ordinary reload or save, rebuild the visible projection as latest durable base plus only still-pending eligible operations in order. Never overlay pending operations already included in the durable base. Apply epoch revocation and refresh caches even on a clean/no-dirty save. Failed read/validation must preserve the last valid base and pending operations, without relabeling their observation as current.

One durable file does not automatically produce a coherent two-store in-memory read. Current `hostWillAppear()` reloads dictionary and touch separately (KeyboardController.swift:352-353); reset can occur between those calls. Choose a shared authority snapshot/coordinator that refreshes both projections consistently, or explicitly limit the claim to durable reset atomicity and per-observation coherence. Specify whether a dictionary-only or touch-only reset revokes just that store while preserving the other's pending operations.

A remote reset cannot instantly remove already-cached suggestions with zero per-letter observation. State the permitted visibility boundary: successful appearance/reload/kept-word observation refreshes the relevant model and caches; failed busy observation may leave old suggestions visible until retry. Do not imply immediate global in-memory erasure on the success alert.

### R3 — Make retry identity and local concurrency explicit

“Commit once/remove the committed prefix” (line 15) needs a state machine, not just intent. Two background requests can both capture pending prefix [a,b] before the first commits; a serial I/O queue then executes them in order but can apply [a,b] twice. Rename-to-success discipline solves returned filesystem failure ambiguity, not duplicate captured work or concurrent mutation/acknowledgement.

Freeze a single owner for local mutable state, sequence-tag operations, allow at most one in-flight commit per authority (or use the bounded token acknowledgement protocol above), and reconcile acknowledgements with later appended records and epoch changes. Never synchronously dispatch onto an already-executing owner queue. Bound queued closures/snapshots as well as pending records: coalesce repeated background-save requests into one scheduled/in-flight attempt and a bounded retry flag, not an unbounded FIFO of copies.

Specify save results for empty, all-revoked, partially eligible, busy, invalid authority, and no-URL cases. A failed pre-rename attempt retains the exact operation identities, values, order, and local eligibility information; a retry applies against a fresh durable base. A successful rename followed by delayed acknowledgement must never reapply its delta. A failed clear leaves all epochs/payload and local pending identities intact. Successful reset revocation must be reflected when old background closures eventually execute; checking epochs at enqueue time is insufficient.

### R4 — Limit liveness claims and include all contention paths

`flock(LOCK_NB)` bounds lock acquisition, not `open`, read/decode, encoding, fsync, rename, memory allocation, or a synchronous dispatch behind another I/O task. Current PersonalDictionary uses `files.sync` even to check pending state; TouchModel has a separate write queue. Reusing those patterns can make the UI wait behind a suspended/in-flight local holder before it reaches the nonblocking flock.

Specify a nonblocking process-local admission/coordinator path: if local execution is occupied, return busy promptly rather than waiting behind its filesystem work. Keep actual background saving active. A capacity flush must use the same rule. Bound retry scheduling and work per kept word; no per-letter observation, stamp checking, registry work, or whole-file work. A storage operation already started still has no hard wall-clock bound; measure its cost and avoid claiming otherwise. If clear must be asynchronous for UI responsiveness, its eventual result must still mean completed rename, never enqueue acceptance.

A stopped global-lock holder prevents all cooperating commits until it resumes or exits. Returning busy is correct and responsive, but is not eventual reset progress under indefinite suspension. Promise eventual progress only under eventual lock availability and scheduled retries; no fairness guarantee follows from flock alone. Native suspension/expiring-activity behavior remains unproved here. Never break the lock or expire ownership to create progress.

### R5 — Complete atomic write, migration, and authority identity rules

Line 17's pre-rename failure discipline is sound for process-death scope if all filesystem errors and short writes/EINTR are handled before rename. Precompute anything fallible needed for the successful result, treat post-rename stat/close/cleanup errors as postcommit diagnostics rather than failure, and publish acknowledgement/cache state without applying the operation again. Preserve the current file-protection policy and define directory/lock/temp permissions; a POSIX writer must not silently drop the existing protection option. The permission and availability of lock creation must be part of the error result.

Unique temporary files plus “clean up only this attempted temp” do not bound crash debris. Repeated kills after temp creation can leave unlimited files. Define a bounded per-owner temp scheme with safe reclamation under proved slot ownership, or another exact bounded crash-cleanup design. Never delete an active owner's temp based on age. Freeze namespace identity, slot generation, collision handling, and cleanup ordering.

The contract does not define first initialization, concurrent migration, malformed/missing legacy components, missing/corrupt v2 after prior migration, or unsupported versions. These are required pre-coding decisions:

- Read both legacy stores and establish the first v2 snapshot in one transaction under the shared lock. Concurrent first participants must not invent different durable epochs or double-import data. Do not import stale initializer snapshots after acquiring the lock.
- Distinguish never existed/ENOENT, protected or unreadable/EACCES, malformed JSON, semantically invalid values, and unknown version. A failed read must not be interpreted as empty successful reset/migration. Freeze whether malformed legacy data blocks migration or is explicitly reported as omitted; never silently succeed after dropping a valid sibling.
- After v2 establishment, never reimport the legacy touch file. “While v2 authority exists” is too weak: deletion/loss of v2 must not resurrect pre-reset learning. Supply a durable migrated/fail-closed marker or explicitly justified equivalent, including initial migration crash cases. A corrupt/unknown v2 must not fall back to v1.
- Current PersonalDictionary loader does not reject non-v1 versions and its writer uses an unlocked v1 snapshot. A still-running old app/extension can overwrite `PersonalDictionary.json`, bypassing the new lock and destroying v2/touch/reset state. Restrict and enforce a rollout where all old writers are retired before migration, or move the new authority to a dedicated namespace that old binaries cannot overwrite and fence legacy imports. “All participants cooperate” is a valid conditional proof, but the actual mixed-version migration limitation must be explicit. A new lock cannot compel old code to cooperate.
- Define exact canonical sibling mapping for each constructor and standalone URL, including existing canonical `TouchModel.json` containing v1 touch data when dictionary is absent. Reject alias/symlink/path mismatches or canonicalize them identically before lock selection. No same file through different locks. Mixed nil, unequal parent directories, and unsupported two-store pairs fail without mutation.
- Define local-only reset of actual existing objects. Constructing two fresh nil-URL stores and clearing them does not erase an existing nil-URL keyboard. AppModel must treat nil group URLs as inaccessible failure as proposed, regardless of any library local-only mode.

### R6 — Freeze ordinary merge, validation, and touch replay outcomes

A merge commits deltas/operations against the latest base, never a full stale snapshot. Spell out dictionary count addition, pin-only behavior when the latest base has no entry, lastUsed policy, normalized block/unblock, pair target removal, and deterministic pruning order. Existing pin creates a count-1 entry when missing; preserve or explicitly change this. Forget currently removes pairs targeting the normalized key, not histories containing it; the contract should preserve that distinction. Decide how explicit blocks interact with transient contacts/replacements without removing transient data.

Touch learning is noncommutative. At TouchModel.swift:115 the current offset vector is computed once at word start, then reused for every sample, while decay updates sums sequentially. Replay must preserve that word grouping, sample order, repeated-letter decay, and once-per-word reference offsets. Summing aggregate deltas or recomputing the clamp center after each sample changes behavior. Keep raw numeric samples local; do not persist text or sample sequences as a shortcut to retry identity.

Define over-40-sample handling without truncating an accepted record, guard isLearning/private-field policy before observation/registration, and validate slot range and all finite values before mutation. Freeze envelope validation for dictionary counts/pair counts, totals/overflow, key lengths, revision/sequence overflow, touch array dimensions, nonnegative bounded weights, sums/offset plausibility, and version/epoch/registrar consistency. A maximum record count alone does not provide a finite byte/work bound for arbitrary legacy JSON; bound reads/decode as well. Do not partially apply invalid authority data.

### R7 — Make the actual UI outcome a release dependency

Current AppModel.swift:229-233 clears separate stores and returns no result; PulpoKeyboardApp.swift:172-174 unconditionally sets success. The proposed Bool/error dependency must be implemented and reviewed by the owning parent before claiming this feature works. A message saying the change was requested is not integration evidence.

The next preflight must name acceptance outcomes: success alert only after the combined replacement; busy/permission/invalid-storage failures show a retryable failure and leave success false; retry clears a prior error on actual success; failed reset retains both durable stores and pending learning. Freeze the async completion behavior if adopted. Also cover explicit forget UI if forget becomes synchronous/fallible: KeyboardController.swift:632-636 currently ignores its outcome and immediately refreshes suggestions.

Current AppGroup selects extension fallback stores when shared storage is unavailable. Even when the app can clear its group URL, it cannot thereby prove it erased historical extension fallback data. Scope the UI's success wording to reachable shared learning, or provide a separate approved reachable-domain/fallback erasure protocol. Do not claim a hidden fallback was erased from group success. No ordinary preview lifecycle defect is established; do not use this finding to justify unrelated engine/preview edits. Existing appearance refresh is evidence of a path, not native proof that every live preview immediately refreshes after reset.

## Required next-preflight verification obligations

These are development/public-path protocol cases, not blind payload oracles. Preserve the frozen R29 result and independent blind-author separation.

1. Separate-process deterministic barriers before acquisition, after lock acquisition, and before rename. Cover both stores, not just two objects on one serial queue. Under a held lock the second transaction is busy/serialized; an old inside-write schedule is not a passing race reproduction.
2. Stop/resume and kill a global holder. Prove prompt busy without local queue waiting; no lock stealing; retry after release. Separately stop/kill registrar slot owners. Suspension retains identity; death permits safe slot reclamation; same-process objects cannot reclaim each other's slots.
3. Pre-rename failures at creation, partial write, fsync, and rename; exact-identity retry once. Post-rename delayed local acknowledgement plus overlapping save/reload/append must not double-count. Prove bounded pending records, queued tasks, registrar statuses, slot files, and crashed-temp debris over repeated cycles.
4. Same-instance forget then learn/pin before save; another writer's stale same-key learn; unrelated late learn; case/accent-equivalent keys; multiple cycles; pruning; pair histories/targets; pin when absent. Registrar exhaustion must refuse new admission without deleting unrelated valid pending data. Test reset with all slots occupied and with registered operations waiting.
5. Ordinary competing dictionary deltas and touch records match a specified serial oracle. A repeated-letter word near decay limits must match the actual once-per-word clamp algorithm. Reload retains pending operations exactly once and refreshes all relevant caches. One-store reset preserves the other store's pending operations; combined reset changes both or neither durably.
6. Concurrent first migration, dictionary-only/touch-only legacy, malformed/unknown/denied components, missing/corrupt v2 after success, lingering legacy touch after reset, and mixed-version writer limitation/mitigation. Canonical versus standalone, mixed nil, symlink/alias policy, and live local-only object behavior need explicit outcomes.
7. Public reset-result integration including simulated inaccessible group, fallback scope, failed clear then retry, and success/error presentation. Source/static proof can cover wiring; actual iOS UI/suspension remains unavailable without a device, recorded as unavailable rather than passed.
8. Measure zero added filesystem work on unchanged letter path; accepted kept-word registration cost; full-envelope encode/fsync cost and maximum bytes; save pressure; bounded retry/backlog/resource growth. Keep current ceilings and the specified complete core/data/services gates. Do not substitute small protocol examples for full regression evidence.

## Approval boundary

This review rejects v1. It recommends an exact bounded registrar with explicit admission backpressure, not unbounded per-key watermarks, expiring leases, disabling background saving, or throwing away every late writer. The registrar's fixed limits, ownership/acknowledgement state machine, durable admission cost, migration policy, validation, UI contract, and liveness limits must appear in a new immutable preflight and be independently approved before coding. No alternative is approved merely by reference to this document.
